India urges US to drop proposed 12.5% tariff tied to 'forced labour' probe

Key facts

  • Proposed US tariff stands at 12.5% on certain Indian goods
  • Tariff linked to USTR Section 301 forced labour investigation
  • India has formally urged the US to reconsider the measure

India has formally asked the United States to reconsider a proposed 12.5 per cent tariff being contemplated under a Section 301 investigation launched by the US Trade Representative's office over alleged forced labour concerns. The diplomatic push signals New Delhi's alarm over a measure that could deal a significant blow to Indian exporters in targeted sectors.

Section 301 of the US Trade Act gives the USTR broad authority to impose tariffs or other trade restrictions when it finds foreign trade practices to be unreasonable or discriminatory. A 'forced labour' framing under this provision carries particular reputational weight, and India's pushback reflects both economic and diplomatic sensitivity around the charge.

The development comes at a delicate moment in India-US trade relations. Both countries have been engaged in negotiations toward a bilateral trade deal, and the emergence of a punitive tariff probe on labour grounds risks complicating the broader dialogue. Indian trade officials are understood to be making the case that the proposed tariff is not warranted and that India's labour standards meet international benchmarks.

For Indian industry, the stakes are concrete. Exporters in sectors flagged by the USTR probe face the prospect of a significant cost increase on goods shipped to the US market — one of India's most important trading partners. Industry bodies are expected to support the government's diplomatic intervention closely.

The outcome of India's appeal will be watched closely as a test of how much goodwill exists in the bilateral trade relationship, and whether the two sides can navigate labour-related concerns without triggering a broader tariff dispute.
