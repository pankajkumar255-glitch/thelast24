PM Modi Visits 1,000-Year-Old Prambanan Temple, Unveils India-Indonesia Heritage Plaque

Key facts

  • Modi visited Prambanan temple, estimated to be around 1,000 years old
  • Prambanan is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Indonesia
  • Modi unveiled an India-Indonesia Heritage Plaque at the site
  • Visit is part of Modi's ongoing regional diplomatic tour

Prime Minister Narendra Modi visited the Prambanan temple complex in Indonesia on Wednesday, walking through one of Southeast Asia's most magnificent Hindu heritage sites and unveiling a plaque marking the shared civilisational heritage of India and Indonesia. The temple, believed to be around 1,000 years old and a UNESCO World Heritage Site, stands as one of the most striking monuments to the historical spread of Indian culture across maritime Asia.

The unveiling of the India-Indonesia Heritage Plaque is intended to formalise and celebrate the cultural link between the two countries, which stretches back over a millennium through the spread of Hinduism and Buddhism through trade and maritime contact. For India's diplomacy, such symbolic gestures carry weight — they frame India not just as an economic partner but as a civilisational neighbour with deep roots in Indonesia's own cultural identity.

Modi's visit to Prambanan comes as part of a wider regional tour. Earlier, his planned visit to Australia was described by The Guardian as likely to attract both a "rock star welcome" from the Indian diaspora and human rights protests — reflecting the dual pressures that accompany high-profile diplomatic travel.

The Prambanan stop, by contrast, carries an almost entirely positive symbolic charge. The imagery of an Indian prime minister at a thousand-year-old Hindu temple on Indonesian soil — unveiling a plaque of shared heritage — is precisely the kind of cultural statecraft that New Delhi has been cultivating as part of its broader Indo-Pacific engagement strategy.
