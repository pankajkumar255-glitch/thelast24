Vance Says 'US Jobs Are For Americans, Not Foreign Fraudsters' Amid H-1B Visa Probe

Key facts

  • VP Vance: 'US jobs are for Americans, not foreign fraudsters'
  • Statement made amid active H-1B visa probe
  • India is the dominant recipient of H-1B visas annually
  • Signals hardening of Trump administration stance on high-skilled immigration

US Vice President JD Vance escalated the political temperature around America's H-1B visa programme on Wednesday, declaring that "US jobs are for Americans, not foreign fraudsters" amid an active federal probe into the scheme. The remarks, while framed as targeting fraud, land in a context where Indian nationals — who dominate H-1B approvals year after year — are watching Washington's moves with growing unease.

The H-1B programme is a cornerstone of the US technology industry's workforce model, and India is by far its largest beneficiary. Tens of thousands of Indian engineers, software developers, and IT professionals work in the United States on H-1B visas at any given time, and the programme is a critical pipeline for Indian IT companies operating in the US market, including major firms with large American workforces.

Vance's comments reflect a broader ideological push within the Trump administration to restrict legal high-skilled immigration, even as the tech industry argues that H-1B workers fill genuine gaps in the domestic labour market. The framing of the issue in terms of "fraudsters" — rather than purely policy disagreement — suggests the administration is willing to use charged language that could stoke hostility toward Indian workers regardless of their individual status.

For India, the stakes are substantial. Remittances from Indian H-1B workers in the US run into billions of dollars annually, and the programme supports the career trajectories of a significant portion of India's most qualified engineering graduates. Any tightening or disruption of the visa pipeline will be felt across the Indian technology sector and in household incomes.
