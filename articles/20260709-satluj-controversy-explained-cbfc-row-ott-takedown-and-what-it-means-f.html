'Satluj' controversy explained: CBFC row, OTT takedown and what it means for Diljit Dosanjh

Key facts

  • Satluj faced a CBFC certification dispute and was pulled from OTT
  • Actor Suvinder Pal Vick alleges a deliberate campaign to block the film
  • Diljit Dosanjh is associated with the film
  • Controversy mirrors past cases like Vishwaroopam and Annapoorani

The Punjabi film Satluj has become one of the most contentious film controversies in recent memory, embroiling Diljit Dosanjh and the film's cast and crew in an escalating battle with the censor board and streaming platforms. The Times of India published a detailed explainer on Friday tracing the full arc of the dispute — from the Central Board of Film Certification's objections to the film's removal from an OTT platform.

Actor Suvinder Pal Vick, who features in the film, has spoken out publicly with pointed language. "Whatever game was being played to stop our film, it is still going on," he told the Times of India, suggesting that opposition to the film goes beyond routine regulatory process. His remarks have only intensified public scrutiny of the case.

The controversy places Satluj alongside a long line of Indian films — including Kamal Haasan's Vishwaroopam and the Netflix film Annapoorani — that have faced withdrawal, bans or platform pull-downs following complaints from various groups. A separate Times of India feature catalogued five such South Indian films, underscoring how recurring these battles between cinema and censorship have become.

For Diljit Dosanjh, one of the most bankable stars in Indian popular culture today, the association with a censored film adds another dimension to his already complex relationship with official institutions. The Satluj case is now being watched closely as a test of how far censor and platform pressure can go in determining what Indian audiences are permitted to see.
