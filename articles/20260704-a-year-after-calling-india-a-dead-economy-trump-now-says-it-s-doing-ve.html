A year after calling India a 'dead economy', Trump now says it's 'doing very well at 7-8%'

Key facts

  • Trump says India is 'doing very well at 7-8%'
  • Reversal from his earlier 'dead economy' remark about India
  • Comments come amid ongoing India-US trade negotiations

US President Donald Trump has publicly walked back his earlier dismissal of India's economy, stating that the country is 'doing very well at 7-8%' — a notable reversal from his previous characterisation of India as a 'dead economy', reported The Times of India. The comments mark a significant shift in tone from one of India's most consequential trade partners.

Trump's original 'dead economy' remark had drawn sharp reactions in India, both from officials and economists who pushed back against the framing. His latest statement, citing a 7-8% growth figure, aligns with the broadly accepted view of India's recent GDP performance and comes as both nations continue to navigate complex trade negotiations, including disputes over tariffs and market access.

The timing is significant. India and the United States have been engaged in trade discussions that cover a range of goods and sectors, and a more positive framing from Trump could ease some of the rhetorical friction that has accompanied those talks. Indian officials have consistently argued that the economy's fundamentals are strong and that the country offers a compelling investment destination.

For Indian businesses and investors, Trump's revised assessment carries both symbolic and practical weight. A US president publicly endorsing India's growth trajectory reinforces the narrative that India is among the world's fastest-growing major economies — a message the government has been keen to project on the global stage as it courts manufacturing investment and seeks trade deals.
