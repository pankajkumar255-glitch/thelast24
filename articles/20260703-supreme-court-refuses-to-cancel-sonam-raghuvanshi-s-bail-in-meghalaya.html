Supreme Court Refuses to Cancel Sonam Raghuvanshi's Bail in Meghalaya Honeymoon Murder Case

Key facts

  • Supreme Court refused to cancel Sonam Raghuvanshi's bail
  • Case involves alleged murder of husband during Meghalaya honeymoon
  • Bail relief continues; hearing involved reported procedural confusion

The Supreme Court has declined to cancel the bail of Sonam Raghuvanshi, the principal accused in the Meghalaya honeymoon murder case, allowing her to remain free pending further legal proceedings. The case centres on the alleged murder of Raghuvanshi's husband during the couple's honeymoon in Meghalaya, and has attracted intense national attention since details of the incident emerged.

The hearing itself was marked by reported confusion in the court proceedings, but the outcome was unambiguous: Raghuvanshi's bail relief will continue. The Indian Express reported the Supreme Court's refusal to stay the bail order, while NDTV noted the unusual procedural circumstances surrounding the hearing, both outlets confirming the same bottom line.

The decision is a significant legal development for a case that has prompted widespread public debate. Raghuvanshi's bail had been contested by those arguing that the gravity of the alleged offence — the murder of a spouse on what should have been a celebratory trip — warranted her continued detention. The court's refusal to intervene suggests it found no sufficient grounds to override the earlier bail order at this stage.

The victim's family is expected to continue pursuing legal remedies, and the case's trial proceedings will be closely watched. The ruling also feeds into the ongoing national conversation about bail jurisprudence in India, particularly in cases involving serious allegations against spouses.
