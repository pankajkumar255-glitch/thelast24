Kerala High Court: Cyber Fraud is Organised Crime, Young Adults and Lawyers Being Drawn In

Key facts

  • Kerala HC declares cyber fraud an organised crime
  • Court warns young adults and lawyers are being drawn into cyber fraud
  • Remarks made during a court hearing
  • India has seen a sharp rise in cyber fraud cases and losses

The Kerala High Court has made a stark observation: cyber fraud in India has evolved into an organised criminal enterprise, and it is no longer limited to shadowy actors — young adults and even members of the legal profession are being drawn into its networks. The court's remarks came during a hearing and reflect mounting judicial concern about the sophistication and reach of cybercrime operations across the country.

The inclusion of lawyers in the court's warning is particularly striking. Legal professionals occupy a position of trust and are bound by codes of conduct, yet the court's observation suggests that some are being implicated — whether as knowing participants or unwitting intermediaries — in fraud schemes. This raises serious questions about professional oversight and accountability within the Bar.

For young adults, the pathway into cyber fraud often runs through financial desperation, ignorance of legal consequences, or targeted recruitment by organised gangs who use social media and job portals to identify and approach potential mules or operators. The court's recognition of this trend calls for educational and preventive interventions beyond conventional law enforcement.

India has seen a dramatic rise in cyber fraud cases in recent years, with victims losing billions of rupees annually and investigators struggling to keep pace with the speed and anonymity of online crime. The Kerala High Court's characterisation of it as organised crime is significant because it frames the problem in a way that demands a coordinated, systemic response rather than case-by-case prosecution.
