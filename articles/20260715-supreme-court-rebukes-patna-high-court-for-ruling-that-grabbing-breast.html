Supreme Court Rebukes Patna High Court for Ruling That 'Grabbing Breast Is Not Rape Attempt'

Key facts

  • Supreme Court rebuked Patna High Court over the contested ruling
  • Patna HC had held that grabbing a woman's breast was not an attempt to rape
  • SC's intervention signals concern over inconsistent sexual violence jurisprudence
  • Ruling carries implications for how attempt-to-rape is legally defined across courts

The Supreme Court of India sharply criticised the Patna High Court for an order that ruled grabbing a woman's breast did not amount to an attempt to rape, in a rebuke that has reignited debate about the quality of judicial reasoning in sexual violence cases across the country's lower courts.

The apex court's intervention signals its discomfort with what it evidently viewed as a dangerously narrow reading of sexual assault law. Such rulings from high courts carry significant weight: they set precedents, affect sentences, and send a message — both to survivors and to accused individuals — about how seriously the courts treat physical violations of women's bodily autonomy.

India's laws on sexual violence were substantially amended after the 2012 Nirbhaya case, with Parliament broadening definitions of assault to include acts beyond penetrative rape. Critics of the Patna High Court order argue it effectively rolled back that legislative intent by applying an excessively technical bar to what constitutes criminal intent in sexual attack.

The Supreme Court's pullback of the Patna HC ruling is unlikely to be the last word; the apex court is expected to provide clearer guidance on how intent and physical acts should be assessed in attempted rape cases, with implications for courts and survivors nationwide.
