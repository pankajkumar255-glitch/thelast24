India orders Meta to remove ads on Instagram promoting child sexual abuse

Key facts

  • India ordered Meta to remove child sexual abuse-promoting ads on Instagram
  • Government action marks escalation of regulatory pressure on Meta
  • India is one of Meta's largest global markets
  • Meta subject to India's IT rules requiring swift compliance with government notices

India's government has ordered Meta to remove advertisements appearing on Instagram that promoted child sexual abuse, in one of the most serious regulatory actions taken against the tech giant in the country. The directive marks a sharp escalation of official pressure on Meta, whose platforms have faced growing scrutiny from Indian authorities over content moderation failures.

The ads in question were flagged as directly promoting child sexual abuse material — a category of content that carries severe legal consequences under Indian law and international frameworks. The government's intervention signals that New Delhi is no longer willing to treat such failures as a matter for internal platform review alone.

India represents one of Meta's largest and fastest-growing user bases, which makes the stakes of this confrontation unusually high for the company. Meta's woes in the country have been deepening: the Instagram ads row follows a broader pattern of regulatory friction with Indian authorities over data privacy, content removal and platform accountability.

The company is expected to respond to the government order under the framework of India's IT rules, which require social media platforms to act expeditiously on government notices. Failure to comply could expose Meta to further legal action and potentially sharper restrictions on its operations. Child safety advocates have welcomed the government's intervention, calling it long overdue.
