Air India Cuts Fuel Surcharge on Long-Haul Flights to North America, UK, EU and Australia

Key facts

  • Air India cut fuel surcharge on long-haul routes by up to 3 units
  • Applies to flights to North America, UK, EU, and Australia
  • Cut follows softening of global oil prices
  • Air India Express separately resumed all Middle East flights

Air India has cut the fuel surcharge on its long-haul international flights by up to 3 units, bringing some relief to passengers flying to North America, the United Kingdom, the European Union, and Australia. The Times of India reported the development, noting it comes as oil prices have eased from earlier highs, giving airlines more flexibility on the surcharge component of airfares.

Fuel surcharges form a significant portion of the overall ticket price on long-haul routes, and any reduction — even a modest one — can translate into meaningful savings for travellers, particularly given the high base fares on routes like Delhi-London, Mumbai-New York, or Bengaluru-Melbourne. The move by Air India signals a cautious but welcome adjustment to its pricing in line with prevailing fuel costs.

The development also comes as Air India Express, the carrier's budget arm, separately resumed flights to all Middle East destinations, reflecting a broader operational recovery and expansion across the Air India group under its new Tata Group ownership.

For Indian passengers, who pay some of the highest airfares in the world on a per-kilometre basis for international travel, any downward movement in surcharges is significant. Analysts will be watching whether rival carriers on these routes follow Air India's lead, which could trigger a broader easing of international airfares from India in the coming weeks.
