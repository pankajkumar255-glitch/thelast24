US Senator Lindsey Graham, Trump Ally, Dies at 71 After Brief Sudden Illness

Key facts

  • Lindsey Graham, Republican senator from South Carolina, dies aged 71
  • Died after 'brief and sudden illness'
  • Was a longtime Trump ally and Senate Judiciary Committee member
  • Had served in the US Senate since 2002

Lindsey Graham, the influential Republican senator from South Carolina and one of Donald Trump's most prominent political allies, has died at the age of 71 after what his office described as a brief and sudden illness. The announcement sent shockwaves through Washington, where Graham had been a fixture of political life for decades.

Graham, first elected to the Senate in 2002, was one of the most recognisable faces of the Republican establishment and a frequent presence on the Sunday political talk circuit. Despite a famously complicated relationship with Trump during the 2016 presidential race, he became one of the former and current president's closest Senate supporters in later years. He served on key committees including the Senate Judiciary Committee and the Senate Armed Services Committee.

On foreign policy, Graham held strong views on South Asia, Pakistan, Afghanistan, and the broader US role in the Indo-Pacific — areas of direct relevance to India's diplomatic interests. His passing leaves a significant vacancy in the Senate and removes a powerful voice from American geopolitical debate at an already volatile moment, with the US engaged in active military operations against Iran.

South Carolina's governor will be responsible for appointing a successor to Graham's seat. His death is expected to prompt a major outpouring of tributes from across the American political spectrum, and from allied governments worldwide.
