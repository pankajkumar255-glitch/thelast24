Apple May Skip M6 Pro and Max Chips Entirely, Leap Straight to AI-Focused M7 Lineup

Key facts

  • Apple may skip M6 Pro and M6 Max chip variants
  • Next lineup reportedly jumps straight to AI-focused M7
  • Represents a break from Apple's usual incremental chip cadence
  • Report published by Firstpost; Apple has not confirmed

Apple may be preparing to make a dramatic departure from its established chip roadmap by skipping the M6 Pro and M6 Max variants entirely and moving straight to an M7 series built from the ground up with artificial intelligence at its core, according to a report published by Firstpost. If accurate, it would be one of the most notable strategy shifts in Apple's silicon history since the transition from Intel processors.

Apple's current practice has been to release a baseline chip — such as the M4 — followed by Pro, Max, and Ultra variants optimised for progressively heavier workloads in MacBook Pros, Mac Studios, and Mac Pros. Skipping the M6 Pro and M6 Max would compress that ladder, suggesting Apple believes the performance gains from an AI-centric M7 architecture outweigh the commercial logic of a full M6 family rollout.

The reported pivot aligns with broader pressure on Apple to accelerate its AI credentials. While rivals like Qualcomm have marketed on-device AI capabilities aggressively through Snapdragon X series chips, and Microsoft has rebranded a swath of its PC lineup as 'Copilot+ PCs', Apple's AI feature rollout — branded Apple Intelligence — has been seen by analysts as slower than anticipated.

For consumers in India and globally, the practical implication is that MacBooks and iPads powered by future Apple chips could arrive with meaningfully more powerful on-device AI features sooner than a standard tick-tock roadmap would allow. The report, however, stops short of a confirmed timeline, and Apple has not officially commented on its chip development plans.
