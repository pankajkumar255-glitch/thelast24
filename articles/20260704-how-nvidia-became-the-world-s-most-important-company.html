How Nvidia became the world's most important company

Key facts

  • Nvidia began as a graphics chip company before pivoting to AI infrastructure
  • Its CUDA software platform created deep lock-in among AI researchers
  • Nvidia's GPUs are now the primary compute infrastructure for global AI development

Few corporate transformations in technology history rival Nvidia's rise from a niche graphics chip maker to the company that now sits at the centre of the global artificial intelligence economy. India Today's deep-dive into Nvidia's trajectory charts how a bet placed years ago on general-purpose GPU computing turned into the defining infrastructure play of the AI era.

Nvidia's chips were originally designed to render the complex 3D graphics demanded by video games — a task that required thousands of smaller processors working in parallel rather than a few powerful cores. This architecture, it turned out, was almost perfectly matched to the mathematics of training neural networks. When deep learning exploded as a field in the 2010s, Nvidia's GPUs were already there, and the company moved quickly to build software ecosystems — most notably its CUDA platform — that locked AI researchers into its hardware.

The result is a company whose products have become as strategically important as oil was in the 20th century. Governments, hyperscale cloud providers, and AI labs compete fiercely for Nvidia GPU allocations. The company's market capitalisation has soared to levels that few could have imagined even five years ago, making it one of the most valuable corporations on the planet.

For India, Nvidia's dominance has tangible consequences. Indian AI startups, research institutions and IT giants all depend on access to Nvidia compute, and the company has made significant commitments to the Indian market. Its position also explains why rivals — including Anthropic and Samsung, as reported elsewhere today — are racing to develop alternative AI chip architectures.
