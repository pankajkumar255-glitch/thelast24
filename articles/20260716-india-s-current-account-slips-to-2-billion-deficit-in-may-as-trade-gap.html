India's Current Account Slips to $2 Billion Deficit in May as Trade Gap Widens

Key facts

  • India's current account deficit stood at $2 billion in May
  • Deficit driven by a widening trade gap
  • Services exports provide partial cushion but insufficient to offset shortfall
  • RBI and markets will monitor trend closely

India's current account slipped into a deficit of $2 billion in May, as a widening trade gap weighed on the country's external balance, data reported by The Economic Times showed. The current account — which captures trade in goods and services, income flows, and transfers — moved into the red as import growth outpaced export earnings during the month.

The trade gap, which forms the largest component of the current account, has been under pressure from elevated global commodity prices and robust domestic demand for imports. While India's services exports, particularly from the IT sector, continue to provide a partial cushion, they were insufficient to fully offset the merchandise trade shortfall in May.

A current account deficit of this scale, while not alarming in isolation, is a metric closely tracked by the Reserve Bank of India and financial markets. A sustained or widening deficit typically exerts downward pressure on the rupee and can draw down foreign exchange reserves if not offset by capital inflows.

The data comes against a backdrop of India's trade performance coming under scrutiny. A separate editorial in The Hindu noted export gains in India's June trade data, suggesting some improvement in momentum heading into the summer months. Policymakers will be watching whether the May deficit proves to be a temporary blip or the start of a renewed widening trend.

For India's macroeconomic managers, keeping the current account deficit within a comfortable range remains a key priority, particularly as global uncertainties around energy prices and supply chains persist.
