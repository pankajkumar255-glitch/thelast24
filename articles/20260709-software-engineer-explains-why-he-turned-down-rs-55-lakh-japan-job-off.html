Software Engineer Explains Why He Turned Down Rs 55 Lakh Japan Job Offer to Stay in India

Key facts

  • Engineer turned down Rs 55 lakh Japan job offer to stay in India
  • Cited family, quality of life, and domestic tech opportunities as reasons
  • Story went viral, generating mixed reactions on social media
  • Reflects wider debate about overseas vs India careers among Indian techies

A software engineer's decision to turn down a Rs 55 lakh job offer in Japan and stay in India has ignited a spirited online debate, with thousands of Indian tech professionals weighing in on the calculations behind such a choice.

The engineer, whose account was reported by NDTV, laid out a set of reasons for declining the offer that went beyond raw salary figures. Family proximity, the social fabric of life in India, and a growing confidence in the opportunities available in the domestic technology job market all featured in the reasoning. The story quickly spread across social media platforms, particularly LinkedIn and X, where it struck a chord with a generation of Indian techies who regularly navigate such decisions.

The reactions were mixed. Many praised the engineer's clarity about personal priorities and pointed to India's booming startup and tech ecosystem as validation that staying home need not mean sacrificing career growth. Others were more sceptical, noting that a Rs 55 lakh package in Japan, accounting for purchasing power and quality of public services, could represent a substantially different standard of living than the same figure in India.

The debate reflects a genuine and evolving tension in Indian professional life. As Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and other tech hubs continue to attract global investment and offer increasingly competitive salaries, the traditional calculus that made overseas jobs automatically attractive is being renegotiated — one viral story at a time.
