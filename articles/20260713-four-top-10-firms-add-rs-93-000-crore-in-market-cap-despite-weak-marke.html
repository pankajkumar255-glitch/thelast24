Four top-10 firms add Rs 93,000 crore in market cap despite weak market; HDFC Bank and Airtel lead gains

Key facts

  • Four top-10 firms added Rs 93,000 crore in combined market cap
  • HDFC Bank and Bharti Airtel led the gainers
  • Gains came despite broadly weak market conditions

Four of India's top-ten most valuable listed companies collectively added Rs 93,000 crore to their combined market capitalisation, defying a broadly weak market environment. HDFC Bank and Bharti Airtel emerged as the standout performers among the gainers, with investors continuing to back large-cap financials and the telecom sector despite global headwinds.

The gains come in a week when D-Street has been navigating multiple pressures — escalating Middle East tensions, the onset of the Q1 earnings season, and domestic inflation data that markets are watching closely. Against that backdrop, the ability of select heavyweights to add substantial value speaks to a flight-to-quality dynamic, where institutional investors park money in companies with strong balance sheets and reliable earnings visibility.

HDFC Bank, India's largest private lender by assets, and Airtel, the country's second-largest telecom operator, have both been in focus after recent business updates and subscriber data that have been broadly positive. Their outperformance in a volatile week underlines how sector-specific tailwinds can override market-wide pessimism.

For retail investors and mutual fund holders, the divergence matters: index-heavy portfolios benefit from the concentration of gains in these mega-caps, even when mid- and small-cap segments lag. With Q1 earnings now beginning to trickle in, the next few weeks will test whether this blue-chip resilience can be sustained.
