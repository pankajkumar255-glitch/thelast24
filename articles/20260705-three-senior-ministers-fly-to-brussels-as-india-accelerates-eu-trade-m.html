Three Senior Ministers Fly to Brussels as India Accelerates EU Trade Mission

Key facts

  • Three senior Indian ministers flew to Brussels for EU trade mission
  • Commerce Minister Goyal updated on talks with Canada, Israel, and Peru
  • India-EU free trade agreement has been under prolonged negotiation

Three of India's senior-most ministers arrived in Brussels on a trade mission, underscoring New Delhi's urgency in advancing its economic partnership with the European Union. Commerce Minister Piyush Goyal, who provided running updates on the mission, also briefed stakeholders on the state of India's separate trade conversations with Canada, Israel, and Peru — reflecting the breadth of the government's simultaneous diplomatic-commercial outreach.

The India-EU free trade agreement, formally called the Broad-Based Trade and Investment Agreement, has been in various stages of negotiation for well over a decade, repeatedly stalled by differences over tariffs, intellectual property, and data flows. The high-level ministerial visit to Brussels is a signal that both sides are attempting to inject political momentum into talks that have often bogged down at the technical level.

Goyal's updates on Canada, Israel, and Peru add further texture to India's multi-track trade strategy. The Canada relationship has been under strain due to political tensions, making any forward movement on economic ties diplomatically significant. Engagement with Israel and Peru points to India's interest in expanding its trade footprint across markets of strategic or commodity-supply importance.

The missions come at a moment when global trade architecture is being rapidly redrawn — US tariff pressure, China's economic assertiveness, and post-pandemic supply chain reshuffling are all pushing India to lock in bilateral and regional agreements with greater urgency. The outcome of the Brussels meetings will be watched closely by Indian exporters and industry bodies.
