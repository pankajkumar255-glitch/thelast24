New Zealand PM Hails India Trade Pact: '57% Tariff-Free from Day One'

Key facts

  • 57% of goods tariff-free from day one of the pact
  • New Zealand PM publicly praised the deal
  • Adds to India's expanding FTA network

New Zealand's Prime Minister has lauded the newly signed free trade agreement with India, declaring that 57% of goods between the two nations will be tariff-free from the first day of the pact's entry into force — a headline concession that signals the deal's ambition and scope.

The agreement is a notable addition to India's growing network of free trade deals, coming alongside negotiations with other major economies. For New Zealand, securing early market access to one of the world's fastest-growing consumer markets is a strategic win; for India, it opens a high-income, commodity-rich partner on favourable terms.

India has been steadily accelerating its FTA agenda in recent years, with deals struck or under negotiation with the UAE, the UK, Australia, and others. The New Zealand pact follows a similar template, with phased tariff reductions and early wins built in to demonstrate tangible benefits to both sides.

Indian exporters in sectors such as textiles, pharmaceuticals, engineering goods, and IT services stand to benefit from improved access to the New Zealand market. Consumers in both countries can expect broader choice and competitive pricing as tariffs are progressively eliminated under the agreement's timeline.
