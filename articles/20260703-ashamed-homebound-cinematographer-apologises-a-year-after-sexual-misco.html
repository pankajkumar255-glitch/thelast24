'Ashamed': Homebound cinematographer apologises a year after sexual misconduct allegations

Key facts

  • Cinematographer apologises approximately one year after sexual misconduct allegations
  • Describes himself as 'ashamed' in the public statement
  • Has been homebound since the allegations emerged

A cinematographer facing sexual misconduct allegations has issued a public apology roughly a year after the accusations became public, describing himself as 'ashamed' in a statement that has drawn renewed attention to the case. The apology comes after a period in which the individual has reportedly remained homebound, effectively sidelined from professional work in the Indian film industry.

The timing of the apology — more than a year after the allegations surfaced — has prompted questions about its sincerity and purpose. Critics of such delayed public statements often note that they arrive when the news cycle has quietened, rather than at a moment of genuine accountability or legal resolution. Those who alleged misconduct have not, as yet, been publicly centred in the statement.

The Indian film industry has grappled since the #MeToo movement with how to handle accused professionals — whether to impose lasting consequences, allow quiet rehabilitation, or wait for legal processes to conclude. Cases involving behind-the-camera crew, such as cinematographers, often receive less sustained attention than those involving actors or directors, even when the allegations are serious.

The apology is unlikely to settle the matter cleanly. It raises the question of what comes next: whether the individual returns to professional work, whether any formal process has been or will be undertaken, and whether the film industry's informal mechanisms of accountability are sufficient. The Indian Express report signals that this story remains unresolved.
