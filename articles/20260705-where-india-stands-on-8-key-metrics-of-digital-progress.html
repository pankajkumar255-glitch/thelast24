Where India Stands on 8 Key Metrics of Digital Progress

Key facts

  • India assessed across 8 metrics of digital progress by CNBC TV18
  • Strengths include UPI payments and digital public infrastructure
  • Gaps remain in rural broadband and digital literacy

India's digital transformation has been one of the most discussed technology stories of the past decade, but a new assessment by CNBC TV18 across eight distinct metrics measuring digital progress reveals a picture that is both impressive and uneven. The country has made extraordinary strides in areas such as mobile internet adoption and digital payments, driven by the UPI ecosystem and a dramatic drop in data costs following the Jio disruption — but gaps remain in digital literacy, broadband quality and enterprise technology adoption.

India's UPI-powered payments infrastructure has drawn global admiration and has been cited as a model for other developing economies looking to leapfrog traditional financial systems. On metrics tied to digital public infrastructure and government service delivery, India's investments in platforms like Aadhaar, DigiLocker and the Open Network for Digital Commerce have put it ahead of many comparable economies.

However, challenges persist in areas such as rural broadband penetration, the quality of last-mile connectivity, and the digital skills gap that limits how effectively citizens can use available infrastructure. These gaps mean that the benefits of India's digital push are not yet uniformly distributed, with urban and educated populations disproportionately capturing the gains.

As India continues to promote its digital public infrastructure model internationally — including through G20 forums and bilateral partnerships — understanding where it genuinely leads and where it lags is essential context for policymakers and businesses alike. The eight-metric framework provides a useful starting point for that honest reckoning.
