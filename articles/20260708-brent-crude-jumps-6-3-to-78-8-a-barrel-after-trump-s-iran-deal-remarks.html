Brent Crude Jumps 6.3% to $78.8 a Barrel After Trump's Iran Deal Remarks

Key facts

  • Brent crude surged 6.3% to $78.8 a barrel
  • Move triggered by Trump declaring Iran ceasefire 'over'
  • Markets pricing in risk of prolonged US-Iran military conflict
  • India is a major crude importer reliant on Middle East supply

Brent crude oil prices shot up 6.3% to $78.8 a barrel on Tuesday after Donald Trump publicly declared the US-Iran ceasefire finished and dismissed any diplomatic deal as a 'waste of time', triggering an immediate and significant reaction across global energy markets.

The spike reflects the market's assessment that a sustained military confrontation between Washington and Tehran — two of the most consequential actors in the world's most oil-rich region — could meaningfully disrupt supply routes and production. The Persian Gulf and the Strait of Hormuz, through which a large share of global oil flows, sit at the heart of the conflict zone.

For India, which is among the world's largest crude oil importers and relies heavily on Middle Eastern supply, the move carries immediate economic weight. A sustained rise in Brent prices flows through to domestic fuel prices — petrol, diesel, and cooking gas — typically within weeks, squeezing household budgets and adding to inflationary pressure at a time when the economy is navigating several headwinds.

Analysts will now watch for whether the 6.3% jump holds or accelerates, depending on whether military activity in the region intensifies. India's oil import strategy and any diplomatic engagement with Gulf states to ensure supply security will come under sharper focus in the days ahead.
