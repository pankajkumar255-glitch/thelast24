Why Rubio Wants to Dismantle the International Criminal Court

Key facts

  • Rubio has argued for dismantling the ICC
  • ICC issued arrest warrants for Israeli leaders, angering US
  • US has never ratified the Rome Statute joining the ICC
  • Trump administration reportedly considering sanctions on ICC officials

US Secretary of State Marco Rubio has been vocal about wanting to dismantle the International Criminal Court, with The Indian Express examining the ideological and political motivations behind Washington's increasingly aggressive posture toward the Hague-based tribunal.

The ICC became a flashpoint for the Trump administration after the court issued arrest warrants targeting Israeli leaders over actions in Gaza. The US, a close ally of Israel, responded furiously. Rubio and other senior officials have framed the court as a politically compromised institution that oversteps its mandate and targets American allies while ignoring abuses by adversarial states.

The US has never joined the ICC — successive administrations from both parties declined to ratify the Rome Statute — but the current effort goes further, seeking to actively undermine the court's functioning and legitimacy. Measures reportedly under consideration include sanctions against ICC officials and pressure on member states to withdraw their support.

For India, which is also not a member of the ICC, the debate is nonetheless significant. The dismantling of major multilateral institutions by the world's most powerful state has cascading effects on the rules-based international order that India has generally sought to uphold, even while maintaining strategic flexibility. It also sets a precedent that powerful nations can opt out of — and actively sabotage — accountability mechanisms they find inconvenient.
