Uranium Deal And Defence Ties On Agenda As PM Modi Heads To Australia

Key facts

  • Uranium supply deal is a key agenda item for Modi's Australia visit
  • Defence ties expansion also on the bilateral agenda
  • Australian envoy calls it a 'big week of conversations and connections'
  • Visit follows Modi's Indonesia trip, where BrahMos missile deal discussions took place

Prime Minister Narendra Modi is travelling to Australia with a potentially landmark uranium supply deal and an expanded defence partnership high on the agenda, in a visit that both governments have flagged as a significant moment in the maturing bilateral relationship.

The Australian envoy described the upcoming engagements as a 'big week of conversations and connections', reflecting the breadth of issues on the table. A uranium deal, if concluded, would be a major milestone for India's civil nuclear programme, providing a reliable supply of fuel for its expanding fleet of nuclear power reactors — a key component of New Delhi's clean energy strategy and its ambitions for energy self-sufficiency.

Defence ties are also expected to feature prominently, building on the momentum of recent years in which India and Australia have deepened their Quad partnership and expanded bilateral military exercises. Analysts note that the relationship has evolved well beyond what was once summarised as the 'three Cs' — cricket, curry, and the Commonwealth — into a substantive strategic and economic partnership.

The Australia visit follows Modi's stop in Indonesia, where agreements were signed and discussions were held on BrahMos missile procurement, among other defence matters — underscoring a broader pattern of India deepening strategic relationships across the Indo-Pacific in a single diplomatic sweep.
