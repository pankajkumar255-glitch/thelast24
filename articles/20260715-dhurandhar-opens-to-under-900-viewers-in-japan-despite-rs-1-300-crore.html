Dhurandhar opens to under 900 viewers in Japan despite Rs 1,300 crore global run

Key facts

  • Dhurandhar grossed Rs 1,300 crore worldwide
  • Film opened to under 900 viewers in Japan
  • Highlights Bollywood's limited cultural penetration in key global markets

Ranveer Singh's Dhurandhar, which accumulated a Rs 1,300 crore run at the worldwide box office, opened in Japan to fewer than 900 viewers — a number that starkly illustrates the limits of Bollywood's global cultural footprint.

The film's domestic and diaspora-driven success was considerable, placing it among the bigger Hindi film earners of its release window. Yet that momentum did not translate to Japan, one of the world's most discerning and lucrative film markets. The sub-900 figure is not merely modest — it is a near-invisible launch in a country with a sophisticated cinema-going culture and a track record of embracing foreign films, most notably from South Korea and the United States.

The contrast is instructive. South Korean cinema, operating from a similarly non-English-language base, has cracked the Japanese market through a combination of cultural storytelling, strong distribution partnerships, and sustained soft power investment. Indian films have periodically attempted similar crossovers — the Rajinikanth film Robot and select Shah Rukh Khan releases have found pockets of Japanese fans — but mainstream Bollywood has yet to build a durable Japanese audience.

For the Indian film industry, which has been increasingly vocal about its global ambitions, Dhurandhar's Japanese opening is a useful data point. The path to genuine international box office parity with Hollywood or Korean cinema will require more than big domestic numbers — it will require targeted cultural investment in markets like Japan that remain largely untapped.
