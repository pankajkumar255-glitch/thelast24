India's historic Lord's win sets stage for women's Test cricket debate; Yastika Bhatia century steals headlines

Key facts

  • India beat England in a women's Test at Lord's
  • Yastika Bhatia scored a century in the match
  • Coach Amol Muzumdar endorsed idea of a Women's Test Championship
  • England captain Heather Knight bowed out after the Lord's Test

India's women's cricket team produced one of their most celebrated performances in recent memory, defeating England in a Test match at Lord's to trigger a wave of excitement and serious debate about the future of the women's game. Yastika Bhatia's century was the centrepiece of India's batting, drawing widespread praise from coaches, commentators and fans alike.

Head coach Amol Muzumdar, speaking to the media after the match, reflected on the scale of the achievement and addressed growing calls for a Women's Test Championship along the lines of the ICC World Test Championship for men. Muzumdar's engagement with the idea signals that India's coaching staff see value in a more structured Test calendar for women, a view that will carry weight in any future ICC discussions.

The Lord's Test also served as a farewell of sorts for England captain Heather Knight, who confirmed she was bowing out after the match despite the defeat. Knight, philosophical about the result, told reporters that "life isn't perfect" — a gracious exit from one of women's cricket's most decorated players, and one that underlined the emotional stakes of Test cricket at its grandest venue.

The BBC raised pointed questions about whether women's Test cricket is struggling for relevance even as this Lord's match generated record interest. The tension between the game's growth and the scarcity of women's Tests is exactly what a WTC-style framework could resolve — a debate that India's commanding performance has now pushed firmly into the mainstream.
