Bengaluru daycare horror: Police defend arrest of whistleblower, say she was 'equal party' to abuse

Key facts

  • Bengaluru police arrested the daycare abuse whistleblower
  • Police claim she was an 'equal party' to the abuse
  • Case involves alleged abuse of children at a city daycare centre
  • Arrest criticised as likely to deter future abuse reporting

Bengaluru police have come under sharp criticism after defending their decision to arrest the whistleblower in the city's daycare abuse case, insisting that she was an 'equal party' to the abuse and not merely a witness who raised the alarm. The justification, reported by The Times of India, has triggered fierce pushback from child rights advocates and the public.

The case centres on alleged abuse of children at a Bengaluru daycare facility that came to light after the whistleblower reported what she had witnessed. Her subsequent arrest was widely seen as punitive — or at minimum as a catastrophic misreading of her role — and has raised fears that police action will discourage others from coming forward when they witness abuse of children.

Police maintaining that the whistleblower bore equal culpability puts investigators in the position of having to prove not just that abuse occurred, but that the person who exposed it actively participated in it — a legal argument likely to be tested rigorously in court. Child rights lawyers have argued that conflating disclosure with complicity fundamentally misunderstands the dynamics of institutional abuse, where bystanders often face coercion or fear.

The episode adds to a troubling series of daycare and school-related abuse cases in Indian cities that have exposed gaps in regulatory oversight and law enforcement response. For parents across the country, the arrest of the person who blew the whistle is the detail that cuts deepest.
