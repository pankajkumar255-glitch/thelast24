How small-town India is powering the next startup revolution

Key facts

  • Small-town India increasingly driving startup activity
  • Enablers include UPI, affordable smartphones, and vernacular internet
  • Tier-2 and tier-3 cities emerging as both founder and consumer bases

India's next wave of startup activity is being shaped not in the glass towers of Bengaluru or Mumbai, but in the workshops, homes, and markets of its smaller cities and towns — a structural shift that The Economic Times analyses as a defining feature of the country's evolving entrepreneurial landscape.

Improved mobile connectivity, affordable smartphones, and the mass adoption of UPI and vernacular-language internet have collectively dismantled the geographic barriers that once confined startup culture to a handful of metros. Today, founders from tier-2 and tier-3 cities are building ventures targeting local problems — in agriculture, logistics, vernacular content, and financial inclusion — while the residents of these towns have become among the fastest-growing cohorts of digital consumers in the country.

Investors are taking note. The economics of serving smaller-town India — lower customer acquisition costs, less competition, and deeper brand loyalty — are increasingly attractive compared to the saturated metro markets. Several recent startup success stories have either originated from or been built around the non-metro consumer, reshaping the conventional wisdom about where Indian innovation happens.

The trend carries broader significance for India's development story. A startup revolution rooted in small-town India would generate employment and entrepreneurial opportunity in regions that have historically been underserved by the formal economy — potentially making growth more inclusive and geographically distributed than the first wave of Indian tech.
