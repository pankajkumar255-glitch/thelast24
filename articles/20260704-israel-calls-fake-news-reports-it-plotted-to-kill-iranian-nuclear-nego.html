Israel Calls 'Fake News' Reports It Plotted to Kill Iranian Nuclear Negotiators

Key facts

  • Israel denies NYT/CNN report it plotted to kill Iranian nuclear negotiators
  • US officials reportedly tried to warn Iran of the alleged threat
  • Israel labelled the report 'fake news'
  • Incident comes amid Iran's political transition after Khamenei's death

Israel issued a sharp denial on Friday after reports emerged — first in The New York Times and amplified by CNN — that US officials had believed Israel was plotting to assassinate Iranian nuclear negotiators. Jerusalem called the story 'fake news', but the report had already sparked significant diplomatic turbulence.

According to the original reporting, US officials were alarmed enough by intelligence suggesting Israeli intent that they attempted to warn Iran of the potential threat to its negotiating team. The claim, if true, would represent an extraordinary breach of trust between the United States and its closest Middle East ally at a particularly sensitive moment in Iran diplomacy.

The timing is charged: Ayatollah Khamenei's death has thrown Iran's political succession into flux, and any prospective negotiations over Iran's nuclear programme or a broader regional ceasefire are already on uncertain ground. Allegations of assassination plots against negotiators would effectively poison the well for diplomacy.

Israel's categorical denial may contain the immediate fallout, but the episode adds yet another layer of tension to a West Asia crisis that India is watching with acute concern — not least because the conflict's trajectory directly affects oil prices, the safety of the Indian diaspora in Gulf countries, and India's own strategic positioning in the region.
