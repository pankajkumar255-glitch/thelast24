HDFC Bank Names Rajiv Kumar as Non-Executive Chairman, Puneet Sharma as New CFO

HDFC Bank has appointed Rajiv Kumar, former financial services secretary to the Government of India, as its new Non-Executive Chairman. Separately, the bank is set to rope in Puneet Sharma, the outgoing CFO of Axis Bank, as its new Chief Financial Officer. The back-to-back leadership moves signal a significant churn at the top of India's largest private sector lender, coming as Axis Bank simultaneously announced Puneet Sharma's departure.

→ Leadership changes at HDFC Bank — India's biggest private lender by assets — directly affect millions of depositors, investors, and borrowers who track its governance closely.

19:20 IST

HDFC Bank appoints former Chief Election Commissioner Rajiv Kumar as chairman

HDFC Bank has appointed Rajiv Kumar, the former Chief Election Commissioner of India, as its new non-executive chairman. Kumar brings extensive experience in public administration and financial sector regulation, having served in senior government roles before heading the Election Commission. The appointment of a figure of Kumar's stature signals HDFC Bank's intent to reinforce board-level governance credibility at a time when the bank is navigating a major integration following its merger with HDFC Ltd. The move will need final regulatory clearance from the Reserve Bank of India.

→ The chairman of HDFC Bank, India's largest private lender, shapes governance over an institution whose deposits and loans touch millions of Indian households and businesses.

10:27 IST

India's oil import cost falls below $70, but fuel prices at the pump may not follow

India's cost of importing crude oil has dropped below $70 per barrel, a level that would ordinarily translate into relief at the petrol pump for consumers. However, analysts and industry observers caution that domestic fuel prices are unlikely to be cut despite the fall in global crude costs. The disconnect stems from the government's pricing framework, existing under-recoveries, and the state-run oil companies' need to rebuild margins after prior losses. Consumers hoping for a price rollback may need to wait considerably longer.

→ With crude below $70, the question of whether ordinary Indians will see cheaper petrol and diesel is directly relevant to household budgets and inflation.

16:18 IST

DAC Clears Rs 52,000-Crore Defence Procurement Including Anti-Drone Systems and Air Defence Missiles

The Defence Acquisition Council (DAC) has approved military purchases worth Rs 52,000 crore, greenlighting a significant package that includes anti-drone systems, air defence missiles, and naval equipment. The approvals represent one of the larger single-sitting procurement decisions in recent memory and come as India accelerates its military modernisation programme. A separate deal worth Rs 5,000 crore — covering 600 Hammer bombs and anti-tank missiles — is also set for approval.

→ A Rs 52,000-crore defence spend of this scale will directly fuel Indian defence manufacturers and signal to global suppliers where India's procurement priorities lie — with real consequences for the Make in India push in defence.

16:22 IST

Defence Acquisition Council Clears ₹52,000 Crore Military Purchase Including Kamikaze Drones and Anti-Tank Weapons

The Defence Acquisition Council has approved military procurement worth ₹52,000 crore, a significant boost to India's defence modernisation drive. The basket includes kamikaze drones and anti-tank weapons, with DRDO's indigenously developed MP-ATGM — a tank-killer missile in development for ten years — now poised for induction into the armed forces. The approvals signal a strong push toward domestic defence manufacturing under the Atmanirbhar Bharat framework.

→ A ₹52,000 crore defence order with homegrown weapons like the MP-ATGM entering service means Indian defence firms and DRDO labs could see major contracts, while the army gets cutting-edge firepower on its own terms.
