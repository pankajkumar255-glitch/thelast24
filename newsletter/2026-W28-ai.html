The AI job that pays up to $1 million: Why Forward Deployed Engineers are everywhere

A fast-emerging AI role — the Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) — is commanding salaries of up to $1 million, making it one of the most lucrative jobs in the technology industry today. FDEs sit at the intersection of engineering and client-facing problem-solving, embedding directly with enterprise customers to deploy and customise AI systems. The role has exploded in demand as companies like Palantir and OpenAI race to convert large organisations into AI-first operations, and the talent gap is enormous.

→ For India's millions of software engineers, the rise of the Forward Deployed Engineer role signals exactly where premium AI salaries are heading — and what skills will command a global premium in the years ahead.

US Chip Startup SambaNova Scores Billion-Dollar Funding Round

SambaNova Systems, a US-based artificial intelligence chip startup, has closed a billion-dollar funding round, marking one of the largest raises in the AI hardware space in recent memory. The investment reflects surging investor appetite for companies building the silicon and infrastructure that power generative AI models. SambaNova competes in a high-stakes market alongside chip giants and other AI accelerator startups, all racing to offer alternatives to dominant players in AI computing hardware.

→ Massive investment in AI chip startups signals that the global race to build AI infrastructure is intensifying — with implications for India's own AI ambitions and the cost of AI computing access.

How AI Helped Maharashtra Stay Ahead of Cloudburst-Like Rain in Nashik

Artificial intelligence tools gave Maharashtra's disaster management authorities advance warning of intense, cloudburst-like rainfall bearing down on Nashik, allowing officials to prepare responses before the event struck. The AI-powered system processed weather data to generate early alerts, giving administrators a critical window to act. The development highlights the growing role of technology in protecting lives during India's increasingly erratic monsoon seasons.

→ For millions of Indians living in flood-prone cities and towns, AI-driven early-warning systems could mean the difference between timely evacuation and tragic loss of life during extreme monsoon events.

15:14 IST

Beyond Earth: Why Tech Companies Want to Establish AI Data Centres in Space

A new analysis explores the growing interest among global technology companies in moving AI data centres beyond Earth's atmosphere. The drivers include the near-unlimited solar energy available in orbit, the natural vacuum cooling that eliminates one of data centres' biggest cost burdens, and the ability to sidestep land, water, and regulatory constraints that increasingly hamper ground-based builds. The concept, once science fiction, is attracting serious engineering and investment attention as AI's energy appetite balloons.

→ As India races to build domestic AI infrastructure, the space-based data centre debate will shape where the country's next generation of compute investment flows — and how Indian tech firms stay globally competitive.

15:40 IST

India's Top IT Firms Brace for Muted Quarter as AI Spending Fails to Lift Growth

India's leading IT companies are heading into the first quarter with subdued growth expectations, despite the global buzz around artificial intelligence investment. A new report indicates that AI-related spending has not yet translated into meaningful revenue gains for the sector's biggest players. The findings raise questions about when, and whether, the AI wave will deliver the outsized returns the industry has been banking on.

→ For India's IT sector — which employs millions and is a bellwether of the broader economy — a muted quarter signals that the AI boom has yet to deliver real earnings on the ground.

21:08 IST

Apple may skip M6 Pro and M6 Max chips entirely, jumping straight to an AI-focused M7 lineup

Apple is reportedly considering skipping the M6 Pro and M6 Max variants of its chip lineup and moving directly to an M7 series built around artificial intelligence capabilities. The report, flagged by Firstpost, suggests the company wants to make a more dramatic leap in on-device AI performance rather than releasing incremental chip updates. If confirmed, the move would mark a significant departure from Apple's usual tick-tock cadence of chip releases and signal just how central AI has become to the company's Mac strategy.

→ Apple's chip decisions set the pace for the entire laptop and desktop PC industry — if the M7 line delivers a meaningful AI leap, it will pressure every Windows PC maker and could influence when Indian consumers and businesses choose to upgrade their machines.
