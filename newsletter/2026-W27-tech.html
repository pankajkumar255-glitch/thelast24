India's Chip Ambitions Are Real — But Execution Remains the Biggest Hurdle

India has laid out ambitious plans to build a domestic semiconductor manufacturing ecosystem, but analysts and industry observers warn that the country's biggest challenge is no longer strategy or policy — it is execution. The Times of India highlights that turning blueprints into functional fabs requires specialised talent, supply chains, and industrial discipline that India currently lacks at scale. The gap between announcing semiconductor incentives and actually producing chips on Indian soil remains wide.

→ Semiconductors underpin everything from smartphones to cars to defence systems — India's ability to execute on its chip dreams will shape whether it becomes a genuine tech manufacturing power or remains dependent on imports.

19:55 IST

DeepSeek's In-House Chip Push Sends Intel, Nvidia, AMD, Micron and Sandisk Plunging Up To 14%

US chip giants Intel, Nvidia, AMD, Micron and Sandisk tumbled as much as 14% after Chinese AI lab DeepSeek announced plans to develop its own in-house chips, threatening demand for American semiconductor hardware. The sell-off rattled global tech markets, with Nasdaq futures pointing sharply lower at the open. AI-driven chip stocks, which had surged on expectations of insatiable demand, bore the brunt of investor anxiety over DeepSeek's growing self-sufficiency.

→ If DeepSeek's chip ambitions succeed, India's IT and semiconductor ecosystem — and global AI hardware costs — could be reshaped significantly.

08:31 IST

Opinion: Vikram-1 and India's Startup Rocket Revolution — Is This India's SpaceX Moment?

An NDTV opinion piece argues that India may be experiencing its own 'SpaceX moment' with the rise of private startup rockets, spotlighting Vikram-1 as a symbol of the country's emerging commercial space launch ecosystem. Skyroot Aerospace's Vikram-1 represents a new generation of privately built Indian launch vehicles seeking to compete in the global small-satellite launch market. The piece reflects a wider debate about whether India's new space policy and startup-friendly framework can produce globally competitive private launch companies akin to what SpaceX achieved for the United States.

→ If Indian startup rockets like Vikram-1 succeed commercially, they could put India on the map as a low-cost global launch provider — creating high-skill jobs and reducing dependence on ISRO for every mission.

03:19 IST

Mahadev App Founder Saurabh Chandrakar Arrested in Oman

Saurabh Chandrakar, the founder of the notorious Mahadev online betting app, has been arrested in Oman, marking a major breakthrough in one of India's largest illegal gambling investigations. Chandrakar had been a fugitive from Indian law enforcement for an extended period. The Mahadev app case involves alleged illegal betting operations worth thousands of crores, with links to money laundering and celebrity endorsements. His arrest is a significant development in Indian enforcement agencies' efforts to crack down on illegal online gambling networks.

→ The Mahadev app allegedly drained money from millions of Indian users through illegal online betting — Chandrakar's arrest in Oman brings India's most-wanted gambling kingpin a step closer to facing justice at home.

21:07 IST

Government clears Vivo-Dixon joint venture under eased Press Note 3 FDI framework

The Indian government has approved the joint venture between Chinese smartphone maker Vivo and Indian electronics manufacturer Dixon Technologies, clearing the deal under the Press Note 3 foreign direct investment framework that governs investment from countries sharing a land border with India — principally China. The approval marks a significant step in formalising Chinese smartphone investment in India through domestic manufacturing partnerships, a model the government has been quietly encouraging as it tries to deepen local value addition in electronics.

→ The Vivo-Dixon JV clearance is a signal to the entire consumer electronics industry that structured Chinese investment via Indian manufacturing partners can get regulatory traction — potentially accelerating India's ambition to become a global smartphone production hub.

14:02 IST

India's silent IT layoffs: Around 35,000 tech jobs may be eliminated this year as AI reshapes sector

India's information technology sector is undergoing what analysts are calling 'silent layoffs' — quiet, undramatic job eliminations driven by artificial intelligence replacing routine coding, testing, and support roles. Around 35,000 tech jobs may be eliminated this year as companies increasingly automate entry- and mid-level work. Unlike mass retrenchments that generate headlines, these cuts happen through attrition freezes, project wind-downs, and non-renewal of contracts, making the scale of displacement easy to miss.

→ For the lakhs of Indian engineering graduates who have long counted on IT jobs as their clearest path to a stable, middle-class income, AI-driven silent layoffs represent a structural threat to one of the country's most important employment pipelines.
